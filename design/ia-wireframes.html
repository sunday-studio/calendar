Recommendation

One surface, many sheets

The calendar is a single surface that changes zoom, not a set of tabs. Everything else arrives as a sheet or a push and leaves again.

  • Navigation. No tab bar. The Calendar surface has a view switcher (Agenda, Day, 3 days, Week), an avatar that opens Settings, and one dock at the bottom: a line for alfather and a plus. Detail, edit, RSVP, calendars and accounts are pushed or presented over it.
  • Why. Agenda, Day, 3 days and Week are one dataset at four zooms, not four places. The other destinations are rare. A tab bar would spend permanent chrome on screens opened once a month.
  • Hero. Up next, drawn as a dial. It is the answer to the question the app is opened for, and it also drives the widget, the lock screen and the reminder.
  • Identity. Gnomon: a calendar that treats the day as light and shade. Past time is shaded, the present is a brass line, what is next is lit.

Design direction

Gnomon: your day, in light and shade

The identity is built for a person who looks at time all day: warm, analogue, exact. The references taught craft. The look is ours.

Gnomon
The part of a sundial that casts the shadow.

Name and mood

Gnomon is the working name. Mood: a well made instrument that sits on a desk. Matte paper, deep green ink, one touch of brass. Unhurried, exact, private. The wordmark is a serif; the mark is a dial with a brass gnomon throwing a pale shadow.

Signature 2 · Light and shade

The day has a lit part and a shaded part. In every view, time already gone is hatched and slightly dimmed, the brass line is now, and what is ahead is open paper. Events that are over fade and go hollow. You read the state of the day before reading a word.

Shape language

Soft rectangles, not pills: the app has no capsule buttons. Switches are rounded squares. Sheets have a small triangular notch where other apps put a grab bar, like the point of a gnomon. States use texture: hatch for maybe and for free time, strike for declined, hollow for past.

Iconography

Line icons on a 24 grid, 1.75 stroke, round caps and joins, flat terminals and generous counters. One icon per meaning. The only filled mark is the brass dot for live and now. alfather always carries the four point spark.

What we learned from the references, and how we differ

ReferenceWhat we learnedHow we differ
Alfather flow canvasesFew type sizes, one hero per screen, tints and shadows in place of borders, quiet chrome around rich content. Every screen is scored against written rules.We keep the discipline, not the look. Our rules: one hero per screen, a serif for time, hairlines allowed for groups, texture (hatch) for state. Palette, faces and shapes are our own.
Shop and reader design canvasesOne artboard per state: empty, loading, offline, failed. Rows of fixed height, consistent gutters, sheets that rise, honest error screens.We adopt the state-per-frame method and the 16 to 20 px gutters. Our sheets settle rather than spring, and carry a notch instead of a grab bar.
benji.org · Family ValuesSimplicity (basics first, the rest when relevant), fluidity (elements travel between screens, no static transitions, nothing duplicated), selective delight (more on frequent actions).We use the continuity rule and the delight curve. Our delight is the dial and the shade, not bounce. Motion has no overshoot.
benji.org · HonkishPresence made visible, countdown borders for urgency, colour that signals the meaning of an action, deliberate friction where it protects the user.The dial is our countdown. Madder tints mark destructive sheets. Friction is spent on organiser deletes and cross account moves.
distill.idCalm, sparse, confident copy. Agents as helpers that tidy things in the background. Works offline and says it is private.Our voice is dry and short. alfather is a labelled helper. Offline states say how old the data is.
